# Today is won in the morning

![Two open hands offering a blue pill labeled 8:50 AM and a red pill labeled 3:30 AM beneath the title Today is won in the morning.](../exports/red-blue-website-hero-1920x1080.png)

There are two ways you can arrive at the same 9:00 a.m. standup.

## Morning one: the day starts without you

**8:50 a.m.** The alarm goes off. You snooze it.

**8:59 a.m.** The alarm goes off again. You admit defeat, get out of bed, and hop directly into your 9:00 standup.

**9:00 a.m.** You are technically awake, but you are groggy and less present for your team. Other people are talking through what they are doing, what they need, and what changed. The information may be useful. You are just receiving it before you have had a chance to think about your own day.

**After standup.** You make coffee and start working through emails, messages, requests, and whatever feels urgent. These are legitimate priorities, but they have reached you first. The day is already being organized by the people and systems around you.

**The rest of the day.** Meetings, messages, and work carried by other people's priorities fill the available time. The creative project you wanted to move forward remains a backlog item. The workout you meant to do becomes something you will try to fit in later.

**Late that night.** You either push the work into hours when you are less conscious and less deliberate, or you defer it again.

Nobody tried to block you. The outside world simply advocated for its priorities, and you never created the space to advocate for yours.

## Morning two: You Seize The day

**3:30 a.m.** Your alarm goes off. You get out of bed.

**3:35 a.m.** You are still waking up, but exercise comes first. You work out for about 30 minutes, then spend another 15 minutes stretching and doing core work, sometimes with push-ups. The whole block takes about 45 to 60 minutes.

**Around 4:30 a.m.** You shower, make coffee, and transition into work.

**Once you are reset.** You write down the four or five outcomes that matter most that day. They might include a call, a social post, opening an account, or taking one concrete business action. Putting them on paper makes your priorities visible before the day starts revising them.

**The next block.** You define and launch work across several active projects. Right now, that might mean setting up AI agents with clear tasks. The setup requires focused thought. Once an agent is working, you can move to the next priority while the project keeps moving too.

**Later that morning.** You work through emails, messages, and texts until you have a clean slate. Inbox zero can be motivating, even though it is not the point of the routine.

**Before standup.** If time allows, you take a walk so you can arrive at standup with a clear head.

**9:00 a.m.** You join the same standup. This time you are awake, prepared, and ready to understand what everyone is doing. You can look for ways to work together and absorb information that may legitimately change your plan.

Sometimes it should change. Collaboration matters, and other people can see opportunities and problems you cannot see on your own.

The difference is that you have already exercised, put independent work in motion, cleared your communications, and decided what you want the day to accomplish. A change now feels like an informed pivot instead of the day simply happening to you.
